# English LP Review Checklist

Review checklist for English-language landing pages. All copy is authored in English (not translated from Japanese).

## Persuasion Flow

- [ ] Hero section communicates core value in under 10 words
- [ ] Structure follows: Hero -> Problem -> Solution -> Features -> Social Proof -> CTA
- [ ] Above-the-fold messaging answers "What is this?" and "Why should I care?"
- [ ] Each section logically leads to the next

## CTA Hierarchy

- [ ] Primary CTA is clear, action-oriented, and appears above the fold
- [ ] Secondary CTAs do not compete with primary
- [ ] CTA copy states the benefit, not just the action ("Start free" > "Sign up")
- [ ] No more than one primary CTA per viewport

## Audience Segment Resonance

| Segment | What they care about | Watch for |
|---------|---------------------|-----------|
| Developers | Technical accuracy, API docs, integrations | Vague claims without specs |
| Managers | ROI, team productivity, compliance | Missing metrics or case studies |
| Remote workers | Async collaboration, mobile access, simplicity | Desktop-only language |

## Brand Voice Consistency

Reference `/labee-llc-guide` for brand voice details.

- [ ] Tone matches brand: direct, friendly, no corporate jargon
- [ ] Consistent with Labee values: freedom, simplicity, honesty
- [ ] No conflicting tone shifts between sections

## "Translated from Japanese" Detection

Patterns that reveal copy was written in Japanese first, then translated (or written by someone thinking in Japanese):

### Subject Omission

Japanese naturally drops subjects; English requires them.

- Bad: "Can manage tasks easily and check progress at a glance"
- Good: "You manage tasks and track progress -- all in one view"

### Feature-First Instead of Benefit-First

Japanese product copy often leads with the feature. English LP copy should lead with the benefit.

- Bad: "Task management function allows efficient organization of daily activities"
- Good: "Spend less time organizing, more time doing"

### Overly Polite / Indirect Phrasing

Japanese politeness norms (keigo) leak into English as excessive hedging.

- Bad: "Please feel free to try our service at your convenience"
- Good: "Try it free -- no credit card needed"

### Word Order Calques

Japanese SOV structure or modifier stacking transferred to English.

- Bad: "For your team's productivity improvement, this tool is designed"
- Good: "This tool helps your team get more done"

### Unnatural Connectors

Direct translation of Japanese connectors (また, そして, さらに).

- Bad: "Moreover, in addition, this feature also..."
- Good: "It also..." or just start a new sentence

## English AI Pattern Detection

Patterns that reveal AI-generated English copy:

### AI Vocabulary

Flag these words/phrases -- they almost never appear in human-written LP copy:

- "delve", "crucial", "comprehensive", "multifaceted"
- "serves as", "stands as" (use "is")
- "leverage", "utilize" (use "use")
- "cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "world-class"
- "revolutionize", "transform" (unless backed by data)
- "seamless", "seamlessly" (overused to meaninglessness)

### Inflated Phrasing

- Bad: "Our platform serves as a comprehensive solution designed to revolutionize..."
- Good: "It does X, Y, and Z."

### Forced Rule of Three

AI loves grouping things in threes with parallel structure.

- Bad: "Fast, reliable, and secure. Simple, powerful, and flexible."
- Good: "Handles 10k requests/sec with 99.9% uptime."

### Excessive Hedging

- Bad: "This could potentially help you possibly improve..."
- Good: "This helps you improve..."

### Empty Superlatives

- Bad: "The best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge solution"
- Good: "Used by 500+ teams" or just state what it does

### Overly Formal Register

- Bad: "Utilize our platform to facilitate collaboration"
- Good: "Use it to work together"

### Comma-Splice Run-Ons

AI produces long sentences connected by commas where periods would be better.

- Bad: "The platform manages tasks, it also tracks time, and it integrates with your tools"
- Good: "Manage tasks. Track time. Connect your tools."

## SEO Checklist

- [ ] Page title includes primary keyword (under 60 chars)
- [ ] Meta description includes keyword and CTA (under 160 chars)
- [ ] H1 is unique and contains primary keyword
- [ ] H2/H3 hierarchy is logical (no skipped levels)
- [ ] Image alt text is descriptive (not keyword-stuffed)
- [ ] Internal links to relevant pages exist
- [ ] Open Graph and Twitter Card meta tags present
- [ ] Canonical URL is set correctly
